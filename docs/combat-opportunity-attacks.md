# Opportunity attack resolution

The combat API exposes `POST /encounters/{encounter_id}/opportunity-attack` for resolving a D&D 5e (2014) opportunity attack after the active combatant has moved one grid square.

The request identifies the reacting combatant, the active mover, the mover's previous grid coordinate, and the encounter turn token. The mover's persisted current coordinate is treated as the destination. The service verifies that the step leaves the reacting combatant's reach, is voluntary, is not protected by Disengage, and that the reacting combatant is alive, conscious, not incapacitated, and still has its reaction.

Reach is derived from the equipped weapon: 5 feet normally and 10 feet for a weapon with the Reach property. Ranged weapons are rejected. The request cannot grant extra reach. The attack uses the same weapon profile, attack roll, advantage/disadvantage, damage, defenses, HP synchronization, and death-state handling as a normal weapon attack.

The reaction is consumed in the same database transaction as attack resolution and any HP changes. A miss still consumes the reaction. A rejected trigger, invalid request, or failed transaction does not consume it. The attack does not consume the attacker's action.

Player-character attackers use their currently equipped supported melee weapon. Persisted NPC creatures can also make opportunity attacks when their creature metadata contains an `npc_melee_attack` profile. Configure profiles when creating an encounter participant with `npc_melee_weapon_keys` (up to 10 catalogue weapon keys) and optional `npc_melee_proficient` (default `true`). The legacy singular `npc_melee_weapon_key` remains supported. When an NPC has multiple profiles, the first is selected by default; pass `npc_attack_weapon_key` to choose another configured weapon. The selected weapon determines reach (5 feet normally, 10 feet for Reach weapons), and `reach_feet` must match it. Unknown weapons, ranged weapons, and weapons requiring special or ammunition handling are rejected when the encounter is created. Profiles are saved on the creature and reused in later encounters. Natural attacks, multiple NPC attacks, visibility/line-of-sight adjudication, reach-modifying effects, and resolving the attack inside the movement transaction remain out of scope. The caller must invoke the endpoint immediately after the one-square movement event and provide the true previous coordinate; the service checks it against the persisted destination but does not maintain a movement-event ledger.
